Overview

An interactive WebGIS collection of peer-reviewed datasets of historic floods from around the world. The current flood catalogues are NKUA for Greece and HANZE for Europe. Greek rivers and river basins are available as geographic reference layers.

Riverine floods

Riverine, or fluvial, flooding is the temporary inundation of normally dry land when water from a river or stream extends beyond the usual natural or artificial confines of its channel.1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8

  • Damage is not required.3,7
  • Seasonal overflow also qualifies.7
  • These inundation definitions set no fixed minimum extent, depth or return period.1,2,4,7

Triggers and contributing mechanisms

These mechanisms can act alone or in combination.

MechanismContribution
RainfallRainfall-generated runoff.9

Sources of flood records

Evelpidou et al. (2023) compiled the catalogue from six source groups:22

  1. Ministry of Environment and Energy
  2. National Observatory of Athens
  3. Municipality websites
  4. Scientific literature
  5. Research programmes
  6. Newspapers

Newspaper-derived events were cross-checked against ministry material, municipal archives or scientific papers. Information from municipality websites was confirmed through scientific publications.22

In this WebGIS

Records & locations

The NKUA dataset contains 1,992 source records for Greece (1886–2022). Map pins show catalogue locations. Record details retain the recorded dates, locations, causes, impacts and supporting sources where available. Records that share a reviewed map pin retain their individual source references and observations.

Flood classification

NKUA records are assigned Riverine under Water source, following the catalogue’s river, stream and torrent overflow scope.22 The other criteria—onset / response, generating process, inundation mechanism, sediment behaviour, setting and interacting drivers—remain Unclassified. The mechanisms described above are background information, not individual event assignments.

Map & statistics

Search and filters update the map and statistics, which count source records. Map layers control visibility. Display settings let you colour records by dataset, a classification criterion or year classes. Year boundaries and colours remain stable while filters update the class counts.

Missing information

Missing information is left unreported; an explicit zero remains a recorded value. Published study figures and interactive WebGIS statistics are presented separately.

Catalogue & WebGIS notes

Flood classification

HANZE retains its river, flash, coastal and combined river/coastal types. The main catalogue also supplies flood profiles distinguishing fluvial, flash, coastal, estuarine, snowmelt and ice-jam flooding. Other criteria remain Unclassified where the catalogue provides no classification.

Record counts

Counts refer to source records, which can describe the same wider flood episode. Unresolved cross-catalogue matches remain separate.

Dataset releases

The collection combines 2,724 records from the v3.0.1 beta flood-impact database with 237 documented historical floods from List B (1950–2020). The main release was published on 31 May 2026 and is a preliminary update supported by the European Commission Joint Research Centre. List B does not provide precise impact totals; missing values remain unknown. Only historical records are included from the companion release.

Recorded information & geometry

Original IDs, date intervals, approximation flags, types, profiles, notes and references are retained, with the release identified in each record. Main-catalogue losses are adjusted to 2025 euros. Its geometry uses the provider’s 2024 administrative regions; List B retains simplified 2021 regions. Both are transformed from EPSG:3035 to EPSG:4326.

The river inventory does not include every small stream or torrent. These layers show reference rivers and basin boundaries, rather than historic river courses or flood extents.

Layer notes

Rivers

The official river dataset is Ποτάμια Υδατικά Συστήματα – 2η Αναθεώρηση (gr_rwb_greece_2rev). Its 1,322 features represent river sections, not distinct rivers, with 1,319 unique official identifiers. It includes natural, heavily modified and artificial water bodies; their official status is shown in the river details.

River basins

The basin dataset (egy_rb_new_2014) retains its original names and boundaries; it is not a newly mapped second-revision layer. Official designation alone does not establish that every river section was individually surveyed.

Coordinates & map display

Original Shapefiles retain source attributes and geometry in EPSG:2100, with ISO-8859-7 Greek text encoding. For map display, river lines use 50 m generalisation at overview scales and full geometry when zoomed in; basin outlines use 100 m generalisation.

Site guidance

  • Enter historic dates as YYYY-MM-DD text so dates before 1900 are preserved in Excel.
  • Do not invent a day or month when the source gives only a year. Describe an uncertain date in submission_notes so the team can assess it.
  • A blank death toll means unknown. Enter zero only when the source explicitly records no fatalities.
  • Keep rainfall duration and rainfall height separate. Retain the reported units, period and any ranges or uncertainty.
  • Place names may use their normal spelling. Uppercase text is not required.

Include contributor names, affiliation and contact details in your email. Attach the completed file and supporting sources manually. The research team reviews each submission before publication.

What is a flood?

Definition

A flood is the temporary covering by water of land that is normally dry.1,2

How floods are classified

Floods are described using different criteria. A riverine flood can also be a flash flood: one describes its water source, the other its rapid response.3,4,5

Examples of flood classifications grouped by the criterion used
CriterionExamples
Water sourceRiverine (fluvial), pluvial (surface water), groundwater, coastal (seawater), lake (lakeshore) and artificial water-bearing systems.3,6,9
Onset and responseFlash, other rapid-onset, medium-onset and slow-onset floods.3,5
Generating processLong-rain, short-rain, rain-on-snow and snowmelt floods.7
Inundation mechanismChannel overflow, overtopping of defences, defence or infrastructure failure, and blockage.3
Sediment behaviourClear-water floods, debris floods and hyperconcentrated flows.8
SettingFlooding in urban areas, river floodplains, and coastal or estuarine settings.6
Interacting driversCompound flooding, such as river discharge combined with elevated sea levels or rainfall.6

Debris floods are water-driven events with intense coarse-sediment transport. Debris flows are sediment–water mass movements with different flow mechanics.8
