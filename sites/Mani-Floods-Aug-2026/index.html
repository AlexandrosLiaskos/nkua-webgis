Study Overview & Scope

During the early afternoon of August 15, 2026, an extreme mesoscale convective cloudburst intersected the steep, fire-degraded carbonate mountain scarps of Mount Sangias in the Mani Peninsula, Southern Peloponnese, Greece. Torrential rainfall (accumulating up to 141.4 mm in 3 hours with peak 10-minute rain rates exceeding 112 mm h⁻¹) triggered catastrophic flash floods, extensive sediment and boulder transport, and structural infrastructure damage across both the Eastern Flank (Kokkala, Kotronas, Flomochori) and Western Flank (Limeni, Areopoli, Oitylo).

To support scientific publishing, post-disaster civil protection planning, and academic scrutiny, the entire research workflow is decoupled into standalone public data ingestion modules, deterministic physical analysis submodels, and open geospatial data packages conforming to open-science standards.
