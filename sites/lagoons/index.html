Submit Coastal Lagoons data

Submit a new coastal lagoon or a correction to an existing record. Include its location and supporting source. Add documented measurements and inundation assessments where available.

Prepare your submission

Fill in the blank Data sheet in the XLSX workbook. The Field guide describes every column, its units and examples. You can also use the CSV with the same headers.

Leave unknown values blank. For a correction, include the existing id and explain the change in submission_notes.

Site guidance

  • Perimeter is measured in kilometres, despite the historical column name perimeter_km2. Area is measured in square kilometres.
  • The scenario fields describe projections for 2100 under SSP1-2.6 and SSP5-8.5. The vec fields include vertical land movement. Supply the assessment source and method in submission_notes.
  • Do not infer inundation from a single elevation value. Leave assessment fields blank when no documented assessment is available.
  • Boundary geometry can be supplied as an additional GeoJSON file in WGS84, linked to the lagoon name or existing record ID.

Include contributor names, affiliation and contact details in your email. Attach the completed file and supporting sources manually. The research team reviews each submission before publication.

About the data

An interactive Web GIS for the coastal lagoons of Greece — explore each lagoon's morphometry and its projected exposure to sea-level rise under two IPCC AR6 climate scenarios.

What you can do here

  • Browse all 152 lagoons on the map; click any marker for a detail card with a mini-map of the polygon.
  • Filter by location, island, or projected inundation status; combine filters with a SQL-style query builder.
  • Open the Statistics tab to see the distribution of any variable across the currently visible set, with histograms, summary statistics, and bar-by-bar drill-down.
  • Search by name, location, or island; submit corrections or new records via the footer links.

Climate scenarios (IPCC AR6)

CodeNameWhat it means
SSP1-2.6 Low emissions Sustainability pathway with strong mitigation; warming kept below ~2 °C by 2100.
SSP5-8.5 High emissions Fossil-fuelled development; limited mitigation, warming ~4–5 °C by 2100.

Sea-level rise & vertical land motion

TermMeaning
SLR Projected sea-level rise (m) by 2100 under the chosen climate scenario.
VLM Vertical Land Motion — local subsidence or uplift of the coast. Adding VLM to climate-driven SLR gives a more realistic estimate of the relative sea-level change a specific lagoon will experience.
geocentric SLR taken straight from the climate model, with no local land-motion correction.
VLM-corrected SLR adjusted for local subsidence or uplift. Available where InSAR-grade VLM observations exist (~55 % of lagoons).
(SSP-based) On the inundation column, marks records where no local VLM was available, so the geocentric SSP outcome was used as a fallback.

How to read each value shown in the panel. All statistics are computed on the lagoons currently visible (after filters and SQL queries).

Plot conventions

  • Histogram bars: bin counts. Click a bar to list the lagoons in that range.
  • μ, med: vertical reference lines at mean and median.
  • Log scale: applied automatically to heavily right-skewed, strictly positive variables (g₁ > 1.5).
  • Frequency bars: count of records per category, sorted descending. Click a bar to list its lagoons.

What is a coastal lagoon?

Coastal lagoons are usually shallow bodies of water separated from the sea by a natural barrier, such as a sandbar or barrier island. They often exchange water with the sea through an inlet.

Definition source: NOAA National Ocean Service — What is a lagoon?.
