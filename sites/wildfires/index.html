Catalogue coverage

Counts describe mapped source boundaries, not independently deduplicated fires. MTBS event identifiers connect related assessments. Prescribed burns remain distinct from wildfires. A pin marks an interior point of a boundary; it does not locate the ignition.

Statistics apply to complete source records matching the filters. Differences in monitoring thresholds, resolution, coverage and fire type prevent direct interpretation as global hazard trends. Methods and reuse

Preserve attribution with downloads.

All 137,028 downloaded source shapes are retained as records. Source geometries are converted to WGS84; representative points supply map anchors. Valid polygons are measured on the WGS84 ellipsoid, subtracting holes for area and including inner boundaries in perimeter. 74 invalid, empty or unsupported geometries are excluded from derived measurements. 0 repeated native identifiers receive a source-row suffix while retaining their original ID. Original shapefile archives and metadata remain available at the linked providers. MTBS text uses the ISO-8859-1 encoding declared in its accompanying CPG file.

Original platform text and interface design are CC BY 4.0, except separately credited source data, imagery, basemaps and logos. Source materials retain their original reuse terms.

What is a wildfire?

A wildfire is an unplanned fire burning in vegetation such as forests, grasslands or shrublands. A burned-area boundary maps the affected footprint. Prescribed fires are planned burns and are identified separately in this collection.

Definition source: National Park Service — Types of Wildland Fire.
