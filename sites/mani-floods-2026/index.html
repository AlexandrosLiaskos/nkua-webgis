Field survey dataset

Six locations in Mani, surveyed on 18 August 2026.

Fifty survey points at Οίτυλο, Καραβοστάσι, Νέο Οίτυλο, Αλύπα, Κοκκάλα and Έξω Νύμφιο, with 200 field photographs captured three days after the 15 August 2026 flood event. Every point carries its setting, an observation note, its settlement and community, and its photographs. The data are available from the authors on request.

Mapped positions

Fifty points at six locations, accurate to better than 2 m.

The survey team placed each point by hand in Google Earth Pro, using the field photographs, their memory of the field and Google Street View, and checked every position against Street View to better than 2 m. Points at the same spot were merged, so a station keeps a letter (S02a, S02b) only when it has more than one point.

The source photographs contain no camera GPS fields, so the positions come from this placement, not from the photographs' metadata.

Field photographs

Survey captures from 18 August 2026.

Web previews link to full PNG copies. Capture timestamps remain in image metadata. Lossless TIFF masters remain offline. Two Οίτυλο S03 photographs were not assigned to any point and appear only in the database. Photographs and videos supplied by residents of Κοκκάλα are kept offline because their reuse terms are not known.

A photograph that documents two points appears at both; within one point, identical images are shown once.

Street View references

The same views before the flood.

Thirty-one reference images show Google Street View imagery from December 2011 to June 2025, for comparison with 38 field photographs. Each looks the same way as its photographs from the same stretch of road, with a wider frame so that the surroundings show. The survey team reviewed every pair.

Close-ups, photographs taken off the road and views that Street View does not reach have no such reference. Smooth patches at the bottom of these images are where Google blurs out its own car. The imagery is older than the flood by as much as 15 years, so some differences are not flood damage.

Settlements and communities

Official names and codes.

Each location is named after the settlement where the observations were made, with the official names, codes and 2021 population of the Hellenic Statistical Authority (ELSTAT), Population-Housing Census 2021. Community boundaries come from Eurostat GISCO, Local Administrative Units 2024.

Submit flood observations

Submit an observation of the 15 August 2026 flood in Mani, or a correction to an existing placemark. Use one row per observation point and describe how its position was obtained.

Prepare your submission

Fill in the blank Data sheet in the XLSX workbook. The Field guide describes every column, its format and an example. You can also use the CSV with the same headers.

Leave unknown values blank. For a correction, include the existing placemark code (for example S03b) and explain the change in submission_notes.

Observation guidance

  • Record positions as WGS84 decimal degrees and state whether they come from a GPS receiver, a phone, or a map such as Google Earth.
  • Describe what you observed, such as erosion, deposited sediment, debris, high-water marks or damage to structures. Do not estimate depths or discharges you did not measure.
  • Name each attached photograph in photo_files and state whether it may be published under CC BY 4.0.

Include contributor names, affiliation and contact details in your email. Attach the completed file and photographs manually. The research team reviews each submission before publication.

What is a flash flood?

A flash flood is a flood that develops within a few hours of intense, short-lived rainfall, usually in small and steep catchments. Water rises and recedes quickly and can carry large amounts of sediment and debris, which makes these floods especially destructive.

Definition source: Gaume et al. (2009), Journal of Hydrology.
